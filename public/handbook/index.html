PL Composer

A painting app for your hand. Everything it does, in the order you will use it.

Tap any picture to enlarge it. Pinch or scroll to zoom, drag to move.

1Start here

What PL Composer is

A scoring surface for PL video. You build a piece out of shots, give each shot its sources from the library, wire how the shot is made in a graph, and build a spec: a short markdown file that says exactly what to render. The board composes; the renderer (compose_render.py, in OpenMontage, projects, pl-curves) makes the pixels. Nothing in this app renders.

Good to know The spec is the product. Everything on the screen exists to write it correctly.
Good to know Your pieces are kept in this browser. Download the spec when a piece is done, and keep that.
Good to know PL Composer also runs as a program on Windows: the same composer, reading the library off disk and running the render itself. This handbook is written from the board; the program keeps the same rules.

The screen

Three parts: a strip along the top for the piece, the Library down the left, and the spine of shot cards across the middle. When you open a graph the spine folds into a strip and the graph fills the space under it.

The top strip holds the piece name (type in it to rename), the piece-wide transition, the pieces button (how many you have, and the list), the counts (shots, duration, fps, resolution) and Build spec.

The Library is every asset the index knows, with a filter and a chip per catalog. The spine is one card per shot, in order. The dashed plus at the end adds a shot.

Good to know A gold border marks the selected shot. That is the shot an asset attaches to when you tap it in the Library.

Your first piece

Seven steps from an empty page to a file the renderer can read.

How to

  1. Tap Add shot. A card appears, called untitled shot, four seconds long.
  2. Give it a name. The name is the only readable label in the spec.
  3. Tap an asset in the Library. It attaches to the card, and the card shows it.
  4. Tap a second asset. The shot now has a base and an overlay, blended screen. Open the graph (the fork on the card) to see the wiring, or leave it: the default wiring is real.
  5. Add more shots the same way. Set each duration.
  6. Tap Build spec. Read it, then Download.
  7. Render it: python compose_render.py your-piece.spec.md, in OpenMontage, projects, pl-curves.

On a phone

On a phone (a window narrower than about 770 pixels) the Library is hidden and the cards stack top to bottom. A graph is never drawn on a phone. Select a card and its graph shows as a numbered list instead.

How to

  1. The top strip, the cards, the transition pickers and Build spec all work.
  2. The fork button is not offered, because there is no canvas to open.
  3. Under a selected card, graph lists the operations in the order they will be written, each with its out name. A shot with no graph of its own says so: default wiring, open this shot on a desktop to score it by hand.
Good to know Attaching needs the Library, so a shot gets its sources on the desktop.

2The graph

Opening a graph

Every card has a graph: how that one shot gets made. Tap the fork on a card and the graph opens under the spine. The chrome stays cream; the canvas is a dark well, because the pieces in it are glowing lines on black, and a cream ground would make every thumbnail lie.

How to

  1. Tap the fork on a card. The spine folds to a strip and the graph fills the space below.
  2. The header names the shot, counts the operations that will reach the spec, and holds Add node and Close graph.
  3. Tap the fork again, or Close graph, to go back to the full spine.
  4. One graph per card. A graph never holds another shot.
Good to know The count in the header is of operations that will be written. A node that is not wired up is not counted, and is not written.
Good to know The graph opens fitted to the window, zoomed out as far as it needs to show everything.

A node

Every node is the same shape: a header, one row per input, and a bottom row with its output.

The dot in the headerThe colour of what the node puts out
The nameThe asset name for a Source; the node kind for the rest
The type at the right of the headerWhat its output is: MEDIA, VALUE, SHOT and so on
An input rowA hollow dot on the left in the colour of what it takes, and the input name
The bottom rowThe out name (v_energy, m_bed) or a one-line digest of the settings (screen, 1.00), and the filled output dot on the right
The Shot nodeHas no output. It says: composites in wired order
Good to know Hover a dot and it says what it takes, or what it carries.
Good to know A Source whose asset is no longer in the index says missing asset, and nothing can be wired from it until the index is published again.

Wiring, and the types

Every socket has a type, and only matching types connect. That is the whole discipline of the graph: get the types right and the spec writes itself.

How to

  1. Drag from the filled dot on the right of a node to a hollow dot on the left of another.
  2. A wire that does not fit will not take. Three rules: the type must be one the input accepts, an input holds one wire (only the layers input of the Shot node takes many), and no loop is allowed, because a loop has no order to render in.
  3. To change a wire, remove it (tap it, Backspace) and draw it again.
Media (gold)File-backed pixels: stills and video. Baked.
Curve (violet)A curves-library element. Parametric, not baked, so it can be driven. That is why it is not Media.
Audio (green)A track. The only source that produces motion from nothing.
Value (slate)A number, constant or changing over time. An envelope, a slider and an LFO are all Value.
Model (amber)Geometry: a .blend, .glb or .fbx. Not pixels yet. Render it to get Media; that stage is never implicit.
Path (umber)A tracer job: his pen path over his art. Not pixels yet. A look renders it; the drawing order is his and is never redrawn. Arriving.
Shot (red)What a card resolves to. Exactly one per card.
Spec (ink)The end: the markdown.

The side panel

Select a node and the panel on the right shows what it is and lets you set it. It needs a wide window; on a narrow one it stays hidden.

How to

  1. The name, then one line on what the node is for. For a Source, its path.
  2. The out name, when the node has one.
  3. Its settings: a number box, a list, or a range (two numbers, from and to). A change is kept at once.
  4. Delete node removes an operation node and its wires. A Source and the Shot cannot be deleted here.
Good to know With nothing selected the panel says what to do: select a node to edit it, drag from a socket to wire it.

The default wiring

A shot you have not scored by hand still has a real graph. It is derived from the sources on the card, laid out and editable, and it is what the spec writes until you change it.

The first visual sourceThe base
Every visual source after itAn overlay on that base, blended screen at full opacity
A modelRendered first (Blender Render), then treated as a layer after the real footage
An audio trackIts low band is extracted (Envelope Extract), remapped to 0.35 to 1 (Remap), and bound to the opacity of every overlay
One source aloneStill one Layer: a base with no overlay, so every shot has the same shape for the renderer
Good to know Screen is the default because the curve library is glowing linework on black, and screen drops black to nothing. A large class of composites needs no matting at all.
Good to know The moment you move, add or wire anything, the graph is saved with the shot. The derived wiring is not used again for that shot.

Order and names

The spec is written in an order the renderer can follow: renders first, then the value chain, then the layers.

How to

  1. Layers composite in the order they are wired into the Shot node. Wire the one you want on top last.
  2. Out names are given when a node is added: v_energy, v_remap, v_const, m_render, m_trace, m_bed. A second one gets _2, a third _3. Other nodes refer to it by that name in the spec.
  3. A node missing an input it needs (a Layer with no base, a Matte with only a shape, a Bed with no track) is left out of the spec rather than written half.

3Sources

The Library

The column on the left is the whole library: every asset the index knows, with a picture where there is one. Tap one and it attaches to the selected shot.

How to

  1. The header counts the assets, says when the index was made, and names where a tap will land.
  2. Type in Filter to narrow by name or extension. The chips narrow by catalog; their counts follow the filter.
  3. A row: the picture (or a three-letter type badge in the socket colour), the name, the length as minutes and seconds when it is known, the extension and the size.
  4. Sixty rows show at a time. Show more adds more.
Good to know A black-ink SVG and a glowing one are the same file name until you can see them. The pictures exist because a shot once rendered pure black for that reason.
Good to know Models and the 3d catalog have no cheap picture and keep the badge.
Good to know Durations not probed in the header means lengths are not known yet. Run the index with probing to get them.

Where the library comes from

The board reads the library and never writes it. A command-line tool, asset_index.py in OpenMontage, projects, pl-curves, walks the real asset folders and writes the index file (manifest.json). On the PC the board reads that file straight off disk. The deployed board reads the copy published to storage.

How to

  1. Rebuild the index: python asset_index.py. Add --thumbs to build the pictures too.
  2. Publish it for the deployed board: wrangler r2 object put pl-vault/composer/manifest.json --file manifest.json --remote, and the same for thumbs.json.
  3. Carry his cuts across: node scripts/apply-gallery-cuts.mjs removes from the index every piece he cut in /curate. Run it again after each rebuild, because a rebuild undoes it.
  4. Arriving: roots for the tracer jobs (favs, maybes, svg), the beds and the Generator inputs folder, so every new piece is a pickable source.
The library did not load, no-cloudflare-contextNo index file on this PC. Run asset_index.py, or npm run preview.
manifest-not-publishedThe deployed board has no copy yet. Run asset_index.py, then publish it.
N roots not mountedA drive is not plugged in. The list is incomplete, and the notice names which catalogs are missing.
An id, then missing, on a cardThe source is no longer in the index. Remove it, or rebuild the index.

What an asset can be

Five kinds. Each plugs into one socket type.

mediaStills and video. Plugs into Media.
curveA curves-library element. Plugs into Curve.
audioA track. Plugs into Audio.
modelA .blend, .glb or .fbx. Plugs into Model, and must go through Blender Render before it is a layer.
pathA tracer job: his pen path, his art, the ground. Plugs into Path, and must go through Tracer Render. Arriving in the index.

Attaching a source to a shot

Tap a card, then tap an asset. If no card is selected the asset goes to the last shot, and that shot becomes selected.

How to

  1. The card lists what is attached, each with a dot in its socket colour. Tap the cross on a row to remove it.
  2. An asset already on the shot is not added twice.
  3. The picture on the card is its first visual source: the one the default wiring makes the base.
  4. A source shorter than the shot is named in amber on the card, with how long its last frame will hold. The renderer holds the last frame; the card makes sure you asked for it.

4Nodes

The node catalog

Add node lists the operations. Each has a dot in the colour of what it puts out. Tracer Render, Bed and File to /curate are in the catalog and the spec, and are arriving on this menu.

SourceA row of the library. Not on the menu: it arrives when you attach an asset
Blender RenderModel to Media
Envelope ExtractAudio to Value
RemapValue to Value
ConstantA Value from nothing
LayerMedia or Curve, over Media or Curve, to Shot
MatteMedia or Curve, cut to the shape of another, to Shot
Tracer RenderPath to Media. Arriving
BedMedia and Audio to Media. Arriving

Good to know A new node lands near the top of the well with its default settings and a fresh out name. Drag it where you want it.

Source

A row of the library on the canvas. Its name is the asset name; under it, the catalog and the extension. Its output type is the asset kind: a video is Media, a track is Audio.

Good to know Sources on the canvas are the shot source list. Add one by tapping an asset in the Library; remove one from the card. Deleting it on the canvas brings it back.

Blender Render

Geometry is not pixels yet. This is the stage that makes a model into Media, and it is never implicit: a model attached to a shot is rendered before it is layered.

How to

  1. Wire a Model into model.
  2. Frame rate and resolution follow the piece. There is nothing else to set.

Envelope Extract

Audio is the only source that produces motion from nothing. This reads the loudness of a track over time and puts it out as a Value.

bandlow, mid, high or full: which part of the sound to listen to
smooth0 to 1: how much the curve is steadied
gain0 to 8: how strong the result is

Remap

Rescales a Value into the range a setting wants. An envelope runs 0 to 1; an opacity that drops to nothing is rarely what you mean, so the default wiring remaps it to 0.35 to 1.

inFrom and to: the range coming in
outFrom and to: the range going out
easelinear, ease-in, ease-out, ease-in-out

Constant

A number with no source. A slider, an LFO and an envelope are all Value; this is the plain one.

valueThe number

Layer

Composites one source over another. The base goes underneath, the overlay on top, and a Value wired into opacity or width drives that setting over time.

baseMedia or Curve. Required: a Layer with no base is not written
overlayMedia or Curve, on top. A Layer with no overlay is a base alone
opacity (input)A Value that drives the opacity over time
width (input)A Value that drives the width over time
blendscreen, add, lighten, normal, overlay, multiply
opacity0 to 1
Good to know Screen is first in the list because it is what the curve library was built for. Normal is the one mode the renderer gets wrong: a transparent region comes out black.
Good to know The row under the node name shows the blend and the opacity, so a canvas reads without tapping.

Matte

The base seen only inside the marks of the shape. Not a blend: a window cut into one picture in the shape of another, which no blend mode can express.

baseMedia or Curve: the picture you see through the window
shapeMedia or Curve: the marks that cut the window
threshold0 to 255: where a mark begins. Leave it empty for a soft edge; set it and the row says cut at that number
feather0 to 40: how soft the edge of the window is
groundauto, cream or obsidian: what shows outside the window. Auto lets the renderer decide, cream for ink and obsidian otherwise, and is not written to the spec
Good to know A Matte needs both halves. With only a base or only a shape it is left out of the spec.

Tracer Render

Arriving. His pen path, drawn by one of the house looks. The path is his; the look is the material. It is never redrawn.

pathA Path: one tracer job
lookchalk, paint, velvet, tension, sand, ripple, loom, flute, caustic, foil, extrude, vinyl, shards, rake, frost, patina, blender-chalk, blender-desk, blender-carve, blender-silk, bead
seed1 to 9999: the same seed gives the same grain
outMedia, named m_trace
Good to know The spec also carries fstop and tool when a look has them.

Bed

Arriving. A music bed under a clip. The track ends with the drawing, then rings out.

mediaThe clip
trackAn Audio from sounds, his-disk or sounds, music. Never Quiet Certainty
tail2 to 12 seconds of ring-out after the drawing ends. Six to start
outMedia, named m_bed
Good to know Every mix is word-checked before it is filed.

File to /curate

Arriving. The card. The clip goes to /curate unmarked, with these words and tags, and he rules there. This node is what makes a card on the render board.

mediaThe finished clip
kindgeneration technique, music; traced, music; or cinematic
mediumchalk, paint, ink, wet paint, enamel bead, gold wire, gold thread, light, oxidation, melt
materialslate, paper, canvas, black, kraft, oxblood velvet, black lacquer, sand, pool water, indigo cloth, reeded glass, foil, vinyl, broken glass, pressed board, frosted glass, copper, lacquered board

piece, wordsWritten to the spec when the card carries them

Shot

What the card resolves to. Every Layer and Matte wires into its layers input, and the order they arrive is the order they composite. There is exactly one per card, it is already there when the graph opens, and it cannot be deleted.

5Shots and the piece

A shot card

One card per shot, in the order of the piece. The number on the card is real: shot order in the spine is shot order in the file.

How to

  1. The top row: the number, the fork (open the graph), move earlier, move later, duplicate, delete.
  2. The picture: the first visual source. No sources, or no preview, when there is none.
  3. The title. Type in it. It rides into the spec as the shot heading.
  4. dur: the length in seconds, a tenth at a time. Beside it, how many sources.
  5. in: how this shot enters. Shot 01 has it greyed out, because it has nothing to come from; hover it to read why.
  6. Any source shorter than the shot, in amber.
  7. The sources, each with a dot in its colour and a cross to remove it.
Good to know Tap anywhere on a card to select it. Only the selected card receives assets.

Adding, ordering, duplicating

A piece is a sequence of shots. These are the four things you do to the sequence.

How to

  1. Add shot on the empty page, or the dashed plus at the end of the spine. A new shot is called untitled shot, lasts four seconds, and takes the next number.
  2. The arrows move a shot one place earlier or later. The first has no earlier; the last has no later.
  3. Duplicate copies the shot, its sources and its graph, puts the copy right after it, and gives it the next free number. Three similar shots are one shot and two duplicates.
  4. Delete removes the shot at once. The piece keeps the others.

Transitions

How a shot enters: it crossfades out of the shot before it. Five words, not fifty. Cut is the default and the honest one; most boundaries in this house want nothing.

How to

  1. The transition in the top strip is the piece-wide default every shot inherits. Empty means all cuts.
  2. The in on a card overrides it for that shot. Its empty option says what it inherits: inherit (cut), or inherit (dissolve 0.5).
  3. Every word but cut takes a length. A seconds box appears beside it; the house default is 0.4.
  4. Shot 01 never has a transition. A fade in from black is a different decision, asked for on its own.
cutNothing. The next shot is simply there.
dissolveOne picture becomes the other.
blackOut through black, then in. The longest pause.
wipeThe next shot pushes up over this one.
slideBoth move together, upward.
Good to know A piece with no transitions at all writes the byte-identical spec it always did, and the renderer joins the shots without re-encoding.

More than one piece

The pieces button in the top strip shows how many pieces you have. Tap it for the list.

How to

  1. Each row: the name, how many shots, how long. Tap a row to open that piece.
  2. The cross on a row deletes the piece. A piece with shots in it asks first.
  3. New piece starts an empty one, called untitled with a number. Rename it in the field at the top left.
  4. The open piece is gold in the list.
Good to know Pieces live in this browser. Clear the site data and they are gone; the specs you downloaded are not.

6The spec

Build spec

The gold button in the top strip. It writes the whole piece as markdown and shows it to you before it leaves the board, because the spec is the product and you should see the exact text the renderer will read.

How to

  1. Tap Build spec. It is greyed out until the piece has a shot.
  2. If shots are still called untitled shot it asks once. The title is the only readable label in the file. Build anyway, or go back and name them.
  3. The sheet slides in from the right: the file name (the piece name, then .spec.md), the text, and under it the command to render it.
  4. Copy puts the text on the clipboard and says Copied for a moment. Download saves the file.
  5. Tap the cross, or outside the sheet, to close it.

Reading the spec

The file has a front matter block, then one block per shot, in order. Nothing in it is sorted; shot order in the file is the contract.

spec_version, piece, createdThe format version, the piece name, and when it was built (PT)
target, fps, resolutionFrom the strip
transitionThe piece-wide default. Left out when there is none
durationThe sum
## Shot 01, then the titleOne heading per shot
duration, transitionThe shot length; its own transition when it has one (never on Shot 01)
sourcesOne line per attached asset: kind, id, and the name as a comment. A shot with none says so: this shot renders black
graphOne entry per operation, in render order, with its settings indented under it
-> shot_01What the block resolves to
Good to know Read the sources lines before rendering. A shot with no sources renders black, and the spec is the only place that can be seen before the render.

From the command line

The same emitter runs outside the browser, so a long piece never has to be copied out of the page by hand.

How to

  1. node scripts/emit-spec.mjs piece.json manifest.json out.spec.md writes the spec from a saved piece file and the index. It refuses if an id is not in the index.
  2. python compose_render.py out.spec.md, in OpenMontage, projects, pl-curves, renders it.
  3. The native program does both in one window: it emits the identical spec and runs the render itself.

7The board

The render board

Arriving. A view inside Composer beside the graph, where the recipes the nodes produce go to be rendered and filed. One render job is one card. A card is born when a shot graph ends in File to /curate, and it moves through five lanes on its own.

DraftedA File to /curate node exists on a shot. Nothing has been sent
SentA job file has been written to the jobs folder (pl-tracers-hf, composer-jobs)
RenderingThe render queue holds it. Progress comes from the queue file. A failed render shows red here with the queue note
FiledThe clip is on /curate, unmarked. The card shows the gallery stamp and the file name
RuledHis keep, maybe or cut, read back from the gallery
Good to know Cards never move backwards by hand.
Good to know On the deployed board the lanes are read-only, from the last pushed state. The live board is the one on the PC.

A render card

Arriving. A card shows the piece thumbnail, the look, the bed, the length, who sent it, and a timestamp for each lane it has passed.

The hand-off

Arriving. No session is needed for a card to reach /curate. A watcher on the PC does it.

How to

  1. composer_jobs.py watches the jobs folder, one file per job, and keeps its place in composer-jobs, state.json, so a reboot resumes where it stopped.
  2. For each job it queues the render through pl-render-queue.py, mixes the bed, runs the word check, files the clip through export.py with the card words and its medium, material and technique tags, builds the posters, commits and deploys, and moves the card to Filed.
  3. It runs under the same Startup entry as the batch renders (PL renders.cmd).
  4. A session reads the board at open and acts on any card stuck red.
Good to know No key or token is ever written to a job file, the state file or the board.

Where you rule

In /curate, as always. The clip arrives unmarked with its tags. Keep, maybe or cut it there, and the mark comes back to the Ruled lane.

8Settings

Settings

Arriving. The shared house Settings panel, the same one PL Paint and the gallery carry. Its files are in the app; the page that mounts them is not built yet. Every change shows at once and is kept on this device.

How to

  1. Appearance. Theme (System, Light, Dark). Glass blur and Glass see-through set the panels. Tile strength sets the curve tile behind the app, down to nothing. Text size. Font picks the face for reading text: House default, His hand, Jost, Cormorant or Courier Prime; headlines keep their faces. Reduce motion turns the animations off.
  2. Notifications. Enable, one switch per kind, and quiet hours.
  3. Debug. Debug mode sends the errors and window numbers of this app so a session can see what you see. Remote inspect lets a session ask read-only questions for fifteen minutes. The key is pasted once and never shown.
  4. Keys. One row per key the app needs: Set or Not set, and a box to paste one.
  5. Reset to house defaults puts every setting back.

The desktop app

Arriving. PL Composer as a window of its own, like PL Paint: local first, reading the render queue and the jobs folder straight off disk, with a shortcut in the Psyched Luxuries folder on the desktop. The handbook opens from inside it with F1.

Good to know The deployed board stays up for the graph and the spec, and shows the render board read-only.

If something looks wrong

A short list.

Tapping an asset does nothingThe piece has no shots. Add a shot first; the Library header says where a tap will land.
The graph opened clippedTap the fit button at the bottom left of the well.
A wire will not takeThe types do not match, the input already has a wire, or it would make a loop. Hover the dots to read what they take.
The side panel is missingThe window is too narrow. Widen it.
A Source says missing assetThe index has changed since the piece was made. Rebuild and republish the index, or remove the source from the card.
The library says roots not mountedPlug in the drive and rebuild the index.
A shot rendered blackIt had no sources, or its only source was black ink on a black ground. The spec says the first; the picture in the Library shows the second.
Build spec is greyed outThe piece has no shots.
